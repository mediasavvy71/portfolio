How I lead design.

My role is to turn complex product challenges into focused, actionable work. I connect research with product strategy, guide teams through key decisions, and stay hands-on from early concepts through implementation.

Leadership principles

The conditions for better decisions

Create clarity from ambiguity

Ambiguity does not stop progress. I move forward with what we know when the risk is low, while actively identifying and investigating higher-risk assumptions.

Align around shared outcomes

Connect user needs, business priorities, and technical constraints.

Make tradeoffs visible

Compare alternatives using evidence, feasibility, accessibility, and business value.

Own outcomes beyond handoff

Partner through build, QA, launch, and iteration, not just design delivery.

How I work

The Process: Structured to Scale with Ambiguity

I use seven connected activities as a toolkit, not a fixed sequence. The depth of each activity depends on what the team already knows, the impact of getting a decision wrong, and the evidence needed to proceed. Some work calls for deeper discovery; other work can move directly into prototyping or delivery.

Keep delivery moving while reducing uncertainty.

I separate reversible, low-risk choices from assumptions that could significantly affect users or the business. Teams can act on reliable information today while we investigate consequential unknowns through targeted research, prototypes, and feedback as the work unfolds.

Frame

Align on the user problem, business outcomes, assumptions, constraints, and success metrics.

02

Discover

Interview users and stakeholders; review analytics and existing research; map people, processes, data, systems, and decisions.

03

Synthesize

Turn findings into journeys, workflows, opportunity areas, and a shared experience strategy.

04

Explore

Compare concepts, information architecture, and task flows against user needs, feasibility, accessibility, and business goals.

05

Validate

Prototype at the lowest useful fidelity. Test realistic tasks and risky assumptions; use feedback to iterate.

06

Deliver & Launch

Finalize UI, content, states, accessibility checks, and engineering specs. Stay engaged through build, QA, and release.

07

Measure & Iterate

Review task success, time on task, errors, adoption, and business KPIs. Feed learnings into the next cycle.

Principles: Start with the problem, not a solution. Test behavior, not just preferences. Use established design-system patterns and accessibility checks. Collaborate with engineering before and after handoff.

“Handoff isn’t the end of UX. I stay involved through implementation and measure whether the shipped experience improved the outcome.”

Strategic alignment · Healthcare quality

Aligning priorities and shaping product direction

Product leadership needed executive visibility, quality teams needed granular measures, and hospital users needed to identify failing measures quickly. I interviewed stakeholders, mapped workflows and CMS reporting, and translated the overlapping needs into shared use cases, journey maps, and a scoped direction.

Delivery tradeoffs · Healthcare analytics

Rescoping without losing the core workflow

We'd designed a highly interactive performance-data experience, but engineering estimates made clear it wouldn't ship in time. Instead of forcing the original vision, I worked with engineering and product to isolate the features that mattered most and delivered a simplified Power BI version that supported the core workflow, with a roadmap for the richer experience later.

Research judgment · Limited access

Building evidence when direct research is constrained

When a project starts without access to users, I don't guess; I pull signal from support teams, business analysts, existing analytics, competitive products, and subject-matter experts. I treat those inputs as hypotheses, design around them, and validate with real users as soon as the opportunity opens up.

Decision facilitation · Concept exploration

Turning opinion debates into explicit tradeoffs

Rather than anchoring on the first idea, I typically develop several concepts (AI prototyping accelerates the creation of options) that solve the same problem in different ways, then review them with stakeholders and users to weigh usability, implementation effort, and business value, turning debates about opinions into decisions grounded in evidence.
