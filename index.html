Working Style

Designing Through Ambiguity

I don't see ambiguity as a blocker — it's part of the discovery process. When requirements aren't clear, I start by naming what we know, what we're assuming, and what's missing, then use research, stakeholder interviews, workflow mapping, and rapid prototypes to close the gap. The goal is to help the team make informed decisions with the best evidence available, rather than wait for perfect information.

Conflicting Priorities · Healthcare

Aligning three stakeholder groups on one definition of success

Product leadership wanted executive dashboards, quality teams wanted granular performance metrics, and hospital users wanted faster ways to flag failing measures. Rather than designing immediately, I interviewed each group, mapped their workflows, and found where their goals overlapped — prioritizing the shared use cases first and documenting the rest as a roadmap.

Stakeholder Alignment Prioritization Research-Driven Decisions
Undefined Requirements · Premier Quality Performance

Turning a broad goal into a scoped, evidence-based direction

The brief was simply "help hospitals improve CMS quality performance," with no clear picture of what information users actually needed to act on. I interviewed quality leaders, studied CMS reporting processes, and traced how the data moved through the organization — turning those insights into journey maps and design principles before a single screen was drawn.

Discovery Problem Framing Strategic Thinking
Shifting Constraints · Quality Performance Platform

Rescoping around engineering reality without losing the user

We'd designed a highly interactive performance-data experience, but engineering estimates made clear it wouldn't ship in time. Instead of forcing the original vision, I worked with engineering and product to isolate the features that mattered most and delivered a simplified Power BI version that supported the core workflow, with a roadmap for the richer experience later.

Cross-Functional Collaboration Tradeoff Decisions User Advocacy
No User Access

Designing from evidence when research isn't available

When a project starts without access to users, I don't guess — I pull signal from support teams, business analysts, existing analytics, competitive products, and subject-matter experts. I treat those inputs as hypotheses, design around them, and validate with real users as soon as the opportunity opens up.

Risk Management Iterative Design Evidence-Based Thinking
Multiple Viable Paths

Replacing opinion debates with tradeoff decisions

Rather than anchoring on the first idea, I typically develop several concepts (AI prototyping accelerates the creation of options) that solve the same problem in different ways, then review them with stakeholders and users to weigh usability, implementation effort, and business value — turning debates about opinions into decisions grounded in evidence.

About

I'm a UX Design Principal who works at the intersection of complex enterprise workflows and the people who depend on them to get their jobs done — procurement teams, quality analysts, and collections agents making high-stakes decisions under time pressure.

My approach starts with systems thinking: mapping how fragmented tools and data actually flow through a business before redesigning the experience around the roles that use them. I've led UX strategy end-to-end, from research and information architecture through the design systems that keep multi-product platforms cohesive as they scale.
