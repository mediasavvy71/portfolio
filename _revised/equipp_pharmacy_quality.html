Beyond the Score: Finding the Right Patient Intervention

Pharmacies are paid on how well their patients take their medications, across many health plan programs at once. EQuIPP showed pharmacists which measures were failing, but acting on that meant outlier spreadsheets, Excel exports and notes kept in other systems.

I designed and tested EQuIPP 2.0, a concept for a proposed Premier and Pharmacy Quality Solutions partnership that connected measure performance to the patients behind it and the interventions pharmacists record. Six pharmacist sessions validated the workflow, and showed the real risk was comprehension, not navigation.

At a glance

The problem

Pharmacists could see failing quality measures in EQuIPP, but acting on them meant outlier spreadsheets, Excel exports and notes kept in other systems.

My role & team

Principal UX Designer for a proposed Premier and Pharmacy Quality Solutions partnership. Helped gather use cases, designed the prototype, and planned and moderated all six sessions.

Key decision

Organize the prototype as one continuous workflow (identify, investigate, prioritize, act and document) and keep the six-month trend line that requirements proposed replacing with an arrow.

Outcome

The workflow was validated with six pharmacists. Three of six misread a lower-is-better measure, which reframed the risk from navigation to comprehension and produced eight prioritized recommendations.

1Context & Problem Space

Pharmacists could see the score, but not the path to improving it

Health plans pay pharmacies bonuses, or charge them fees, based on quality measures such as adherence to diabetes, cholesterol and blood pressure medications. A patient counts as adherent when their proportion of days covered (PDC) stays above a threshold, usually 80%. A single pharmacy can sit in a Humana network, a UnitedHealthcare plan, an Express Scripts program and more, each with its own goals.

EQuIPP was good at reporting performance. Turning performance into work was the hard part. Pharmacists described pulling outlier files, keeping call lists in spreadsheets, printing patient lists to carry through the day, and documenting in their dispensing systems. Every day they had to answer the same questions:

  • Which measure needs my attention first?
  • Which patients are driving the gap?
  • Who can I still help before the year closes?
  • How do I show the plan I did my due diligence?

Many Programs, One Pharmacist

Each health plan program has its own measures, goals and payouts. Pharmacists triage by plan because that is how they are judged, but the data was not organized that way.

Performance Disconnected From Patients

Seeing that Diabetes PDC was below goal did not tell a pharmacist which four patients to call, or which of them could still reach 80% before year end.

Documentation Lived Elsewhere

Checkbox documentation could not tell the story of an intervention, so notes lived in dispensing systems and spreadsheets. The record of effort was scattered.

"I want to focus my work where we get the most bang for the buck."

2Discovery & Research Approach

Starting from use cases, then putting a prototype in front of pharmacists

I helped gather use cases from Pharmacy Quality Solutions client feedback for four user types: single pharmacies, corporate and regional groups, payers, and internal PQS staff. The single-pharmacy use case was the clearest signal. Users wanted to see the actual names behind every patient count, filter by program, see the prescription behind each outlier, and keep a documentation history that survived data refreshes.

Those needs shaped the prototype. To test it, I ran remote moderated think-aloud usability test sessions over Zoom. Each pharmacist shared their screen and worked through one realistic scenario without guidance. When they got stuck, I asked what they expected to happen. The rest of each session explored programs, payouts, documentation and immunizations.

The Scenario

"You need to determine which measures need the most improvement. Then find the patients that need clinical interventions in order to increase the measure's quality performance."

Why moderated: terms like PDC, outlier, gap and designation carry exact meanings. I needed to hear how each pharmacist interpreted them, not just whether they clicked the right thing.

3Design Process

One continuous workflow, organized around the decisions pharmacists make

The data already existed. What was missing was a structure that followed how pharmacists think. I organized the prototype around five questions, one per tab: how am I performing (Measures), what is it worth (My Programs), where do I stand with each plan (Quality Improvement Programs), who needs help (Patients), and Immunization.

Every screen served one workflow: spot the gap, find the patients behind it, act, and document. Each step carried forward the context a pharmacist needs for the next one: the measure, the plan, the patient's rate and their intervention status.

Decision 01 · Measures Dashboard

Four numbers that decide whether to drill in

Each card puts rate, goal, patient count and gap side by side, under a pass or fail icon and a six-month trend. The gap tells a pharmacist how many patients stand between them and the goal, which is how they choose where to spend the week.

What testing showedParticipants scanned the cards quickly. One compared picking a measure by its gap to deciding which of three credit cards to pay off first.
Design Call · Keeping the Trend Line

Early requirements and the single-pharmacy use case proposed replacing the dashboard trend line with a simple up or down arrow. I kept the six-month trend. The direction and size of a change are part of judging a measure, and pharmacists need that history to tell whether a gap is closing or widening. Testing backed this up: four participants used the trends to decide where to focus. It also showed the cost, which became the study's main finding.

Decision 02 · Measure Detail

One click from a failing score to the patients behind it

The detail page shows the score against organization, state and all-EQuIPP averages, then tabs for the patients, programs, health plans and insurance mix behind that single measure. Pharmacists can switch measures from a dropdown without going back.

What testing showedDrilling from a measure to its detail "works the way he would expect," in one participant's words. Participants valued the comparators and the status column.
Decision 03 · Patient View

Every gap a patient has, not just the one you arrived from

The patient list ranks people by the number of measures where they have an opportunity. The patient view groups their PDC measures as tiles and lists every other measure below, so a pharmacist can address several issues in a single call.

What testing showedPharmacists wanted exactly this holistic view. As one said, "Diabetes is important but we also deal with patients holistically." But four of six could not explain the "# of Measures" label.
Decision 04 · Intervention & Documentation

Capture the intervention where the work happens

The intervention modal records actions taken, the primary barrier, the outcome and a free-text note. A Patient Documentation tab keeps every entry per measure over time, and a header link keeps the latest data-refresh notice within reach.

What testing showedDocumentation decides whether pharmacists work in the platform at all. One said seeing past actions on his outliers would be "more time effective for me than that information being in my pharmacy system."

4Usability Findings

The workflow held up. The meaning of the numbers didn't.

Participants moved from a failing measure to the patients behind it mostly unaided. Navigation was not the problem. The biggest risks to a pharmacist acting correctly were a misread measure direction, unclear terms and an incomplete record of interventions.

The clearest example: High-Risk Medications is a measure where lower is better. Its card said so. But its trend line sat below the goal line, which on every other card meant failure. Three of six participants picked it as their worst measure.

3/6participants

Measure direction misread

Picked High-Risk Medications as worst. They read the chart and skipped the "Lower is better" label beneath the title.

4/6participants

Terminology unclear

Could not explain "# of Measures," and several mixed up gaps, outliers and opportunities. "I feel like I sort of stumbled through it," said one.

4/6participants

Breadcrumbs overlooked

Looked for a back button or clicked the logo before noticing the breadcrumb. One called it "awfully small."

5/6participants

Wanted to filter by program

Payers judge pharmacies plan by plan. "I am going to focus mainly on what we are evaluated on," said P5.

5/6participants

Triage closest-to-goal first

Described working the "low-hanging fruit" first, especially late in the year. The prototype sorted by rate and had no at-risk view.

5/6participants

Documentation needs the story

Wanted to record why a patient was non-adherent, per measure, over time, without being locked in, to show due diligence.

5Outcomes & Recommendations

A validated workflow and a prioritized roadmap for the next iteration

✅

Core Workflow Validated

Pharmacists wanted to go from measure to patient to action in one place, and navigated the connected flow mostly unaided before any production code was written.

🔎

Risk Reframed: Comprehension Over Navigation

The research moved the team's attention from layout to meaning: direction, terminology and goal sources, the places where a misread number sends the wrong patient a call.

🧭

Pharmacist Mental Models Documented

Unprompted, pharmacists described the same triage strategy: plan first, then patients closest to the threshold. That became a design requirement, not an anecdote.

What I Validated · What Still Needed Testing
✓The measure-to-patient workflow and card layout for triage
✓Demand for plan-level and patient-level views
○Payout figures, which were placeholders in the prototype
○Documentation in depth, since most feedback was about the current EQuIPP
○The eight recommendations, which were not built and needed a second round of testing

Eight prioritized recommendations

  1. Move the Higher or Lower is better cue next to the performance rate High
  2. Add an At-Risk status for patients nearing non-adherence High
  3. Add a program filter to every patient list High
  4. Add Outlier, At-Risk and Adherent views to all patient lists High
  5. Standardize labels and validate terminology with users Medium
  6. Open the whole patient record from inside a measure drill-down Medium
  7. Increase the affordance of status icons Medium
  8. Reorder tabs to Measures, Patients, Quality Improvement Programs, My Programs, Immunization Low
Reflection

A dashboard people can read is not the same as a workflow they can act on.

Participants read the trend charts fluently, and half still drew the wrong conclusion from one. In healthcare data, the meaning of a number, its direction, its denominator and who set its goal, has to sit right next to the number.

What I'd do next

  • Test the comprehension fixes and at-risk sorting with the same scenario.
  • Add a direction-reading task to measure the difference.
